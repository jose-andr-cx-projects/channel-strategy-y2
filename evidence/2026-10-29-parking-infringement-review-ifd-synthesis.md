# Parking Infringement Review evidence — implications for the Information Front Door

**Evidence reviewed:** City of Melbourne, *Parking Infringement Review — Customer Experience Uplift*, August 2024 (62-page internal report; source document held in the approved CoM environment).  
**Synthesis date:** 29 October 2026  
**Workstream:** Channel Strategy Y2 / Information Front Door (IFD), with focus on **Find → Understand** for Pay a Fine.  
**Status:** Working evidence interpretation; **not** a confirmed design decision or verified current-state service specification.

## 1. Source evidence

- FY2023–24: **25,059 parking infringement review requests**; report shows **35% CSAT**, compared with **65% CoM-wide**, and **32% timeliness**, compared with **65% CoM-wide** (report pp. 4–5).
- In the **2–14 July 2024 interaction capture**, **66%** of recorded customer categories were **visitors**, and **91%** of cases originated through **webforms** (pp. 6–7). These proportions do **not** describe all customers who receive or pay parking fines.
- Review outcomes in the capture were reported as **50% withdrawn, 44% proceed, 1% unauthorised to act, 5% other** (p. 8). The 1% is a prompt to investigate representation and authorisation, not proof of the scale or causes of third-party issues.
- The report identified eight customer needs (p. 15): manage expectations; reliable parking technology; help providing relevant information; process guidance; status updates; reassurance; feeling heard/understood; and repairing perceptions of CoM.
- Research described confusion about legal and procedural categories, difficulty knowing what evidence is relevant, and uncertainty about the process (pp. 30–37). Interview participants reported that writing and uploading evidence was straightforward; **this does not establish that all customers find the form easy**.
- Reported EasyPark / meter / area-code issues created confusion among customers who believed they had paid correctly (pp. 25–28). These are issues described in 2024, **not confirmed 2026 defects**.
- The report records customer expectations for immediate acknowledgement, understandable timeframes and clear next steps (pp. 20–23); and identifies demand for progress communication (Insight 5, pp. 38 onward).
- **Sampling caution:** qualitative customer interview participants had requested a review, received a withdrawal, opted in and reported CSAT of **3/5 or below** (p. 13). These experiences are valuable for identifying friction but cannot be generalised to all infringements or to payment-only journeys.

## 2. IFD interpretation: Find and Understand

**Interpretation:** The entry experience should help a person make sense of **their situation and purpose** before requiring knowledge of council service categories. The evidence supports testing an **intent-led** entry model with customer context introduced only when it changes guidance, rather than treating demographic segments or the binary **Representing a business / Resident or visitor** choice as a sufficient universal starting point.

| Research finding | Find requirement | Understand requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Many captured review customers were visitors | Entry points discoverable without council familiarity | Explain the authority, notice and terminology plainly |
| Review language and categories can confuse | Surface recognisable situations such as “I paid but got a fine” | Explain review routes, conditions and evidence without deciding eligibility on behalf of the customer |
| Customers want to know what happens next | Make review information and enquiry options easy to locate | Explain verified milestones, communications and the limits of any status service |
| Issues can involve parking apps, meters or zones | Provide routes from parking-technology problems to appropriate guidance | Describe possible explanations and available official next steps, without promising an outcome |
| Customers want to be heard and understood | Provide supportive and accessible routes | Set expectations for relevant information, fair consideration and contact options |

**Proposed Find → Understand pattern (to validate, not a mandatory questionnaire):**
1. **Recognise the situation:** e.g. “I've received a fine”, “I paid for parking”, “I'm helping someone else”, “I've already requested a review”.
2. **Clarify context only where material:** acting for myself, a business or someone else; visitor versus resident; notice stage and issuing authority.
3. **Build understanding:** responsibility, relevant rules, options, evidence, requirements, timing and next steps.
4. **Hand over to the authoritative service:** payment, review, nomination/representative arrangements where allowed, support or status enquiry.

## 3. Scenario cards for testing

| Scenario | Primary Find question | Primary Understand question | Evidence strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| A visitor returns to their car to find a ticket | Where do I start? | What does the fine mean and what can I do? | **Illustrative**, informed by visitor prominence and need for clarity |
| I paid through EasyPark but received a fine | Where is guidance for a disputed parking payment? | What could have happened, what evidence matters and what are my options? | **Directly informed** by 2024 research |
| I want to request a review but don't understand the categories | How do I explore a review? | Which grounds and information are relevant? | **Directly informed** by 2024 research |
| I have already submitted a review | Where do I go for an update? | What happens next and which communication should I expect? | **Directly informed** by report insights |
| A rental-car company is managing a tourist's fine | Where is the business/third-party pathway? | Who may act and under what rules? | **Hypothesis**; not directly studied in this report |
| A family member is helping someone respond | How can I help another person? | What authority or consent is required? | **Hypothesis**, with only limited signal from “unauthorised to act” outcomes |
| Courier fined while working | Is this a business or individual matter? | Whose responsibility is it? | **Hypothesis**; validate against service policy |

## 4. Boundary of IFD versus service redesign

**Within IFD Find and Understand:** plain-language entry points, discoverability, contextual explanations, evidence guidance, service ownership clarity, verified expectations and accurate links to existing services.

**Separate service/process opportunities:** EasyPark/enforcement-system reliability, review decision rules, processing time, real-time case tracking, proactive notifications, and downstream form/process changes. The IFD should not imply it can resolve those problems without the responsible service owners.

## 5. Proposed validation and next actions

1. Playback three **evidence-led cards**: “paid but fined”, “unclear review grounds”, and “waiting for a review outcome”; use hire-car company as a deliberate **stress test** of audience-based navigation.
2. Compare **intent-led** and **audience-first** findability using realistic tasks; observe whether customers can correctly explain the appropriate next action, rather than only whether they locate a page.
3. Validate content, legal/process criteria, authority to act, timeframe claims and existing system handoffs with the Parking Infringement Review service owner before publishing customer-facing guidance.
4. Confirm what has changed since **August 2024**; use new operational evidence before claiming the findings represent today's performance.
5. Capture outcomes in the project's approved decision register **only after** Dennis/Chris/Mindy and the relevant decision authority have considered the proposed direction.

**Working design proposition (not endorsed):** *The IFD helps customers understand their situation and next step before they are required to navigate an organisational process.*

# # Channel Strategy Y2

## Connected customer interactions

## Summary

Channel Strategy Y2 is about helping City of Melbourne move from fragmented customer channels to connected customer interactions.

In Year 1, the work helped define the direction for customer channels.

In Year 2, the focus shifts to practical enablement: creating reusable service logic, patterns, standards and decision tools that can be tested through real service contexts.

The work will define and test a minimum viable Connected Interactions Framework, supported by service information architecture, so customers can:

- find the right pathway
- receive the right level of support
- move between channels with less repetition
- receive consistent information
- understand what has happened and what happens next

The current approach is to learn through practical cases rather than fully define the framework in isolation.

---

## Why this matters

Customers do not experience Council as separate systems, teams or channels.

They experience one organisation.

A customer may:

1. start with Google or AI search
2. move to the website
3. look for information or a service
4. complete a form or request
5. call for clarification or an update
6. receive a system-generated status
7. speak to a staff member who may need to reconstruct the context

The opportunity is to create shared service logic that helps these interactions work together.

| Current challenge | Y2 response |
| --- | --- |
| Channels are improved separately | Create shared service logic across channels |
| Customers search by intent, but services are often structured internally | Build intent-led service information architecture |
| Customers may not know which pathway or support mode is appropriate | Develop reusable routing and response-mode logic |
| Status updates may reflect internal workflow terms | Create customer-facing journey status patterns |
| Staff may need to reconstruct context | Define minimum connected-context and handover needs |
| Knowledge can differ across interaction points | Strengthen source-of-truth and knowledge patterns |
| Digital, CRM, Contact Centre, Knowledge and service work can drift | Align related initiatives through a common framework |
| Performance is often measured by channel | Shift toward journey-based measurement |

## What Y2 will produce

Channel Strategy Y2 will not be a standalone strategy document.

It will produce practical assets that can be tested and reused through active initiatives.

| Output | Purpose |
| --- | --- |

| Routing and channel principles | Decision rules for directing customers to the right pathway or support mode |
| Journey-based status patterns | Plain-English patterns that explain what has happened, what happens next and when to expect an update |

## Current practical cases

### Sustainability

Sustainability is the immediate **Intelligent Front Door proof of concept**.

The aim is to test how customers can:

- express or recognise their intent
- find the right information
- understand the next action
- reach the appropriate pathway
- access support where needed

Potential interaction mechanisms can be explored, but the work should test the service logic before committing to a particular technology.

### Parking / infringement review

The parking / infringement review work continues as a complementary problem-framing and future design case.

The immediate focus is to refine and validate:

- customer understanding of review criteria
- eligibility and routing
- clarity before starting the process
- how operational or policy rules can be translated into clearer customer guidance

Learning from the sustainability proof of concept should inform this work where useful.

### Off-Platform Search

The Off-Platform Search test preparation is ready, but execution has been deliberately deferred while the website baseline changes.

The current expectation is to resume around **Sprint 4**, subject to website readiness.

This is a sequencing decision, not a cancellation of the work.

## Delivery principles

| Principle | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Decision first | Structure discovery and design around decisions that need to be made |
| Test through real work | Use practical service contexts rather than designing the framework entirely in isolation |
| Start with customer intent | Organise pathways around what customers are trying to achieve |
| Design for connected interactions | Improve how channels and services work together |
| Use the simplest useful test | Build only what is needed to learn |
| Make service logic reusable | Capture patterns that can apply beyond one service |
| Balance self-service with support access | Improve digital pathways while protecting appropriate assisted options |
| Keep knowledge consistent | Strengthen source-of-truth and information quality |
| Measure journeys, not just channels | Understand whether the customer's need was resolved end to end |
| Evidence with caveats | Distinguish evidence, assumption, interpretation and recommendation |
| Human in the loop | Technology can support interactions; accountable people remain responsible for service decisions |

## Plain-English narrative

Channel Strategy Y2 helps City of Melbourne create a more coherent customer experience across channels.

The work is not about forcing customers into digital channels or redesigning every service at once.

It is about creating the practical service logic that helps customers:

- find the right pathway
- receive the right support
- move between channels with less repetition
- receive consistent information
- understand what is happening

The project is now moving from broad discovery toward practical testing.

Sustainability provides the immediate Intelligent Front Door proof-of-concept opportunity.

Parking / infringement review provides a complementary future design case.

Off-Platform Search remains prepared but deliberately paused until the website baseline is ready.

Together, these activities will help turn the Channel Strategy direction into reusable organisational capability.
